Section 00 · OrientationMission profile

A standard JEPA has one predictor, and a human chose its shape. This machine grows extra predictors while it trains, and nobody chooses their shape — the shape falls out of the geometry of the model's own uncertainty.

The claim the whole repository exists to test is a single sentence: predictive modules should emerge from the dynamics of the system's own exploration of what it does not yet know. Everything below is the machinery that turns that sentence into running code.

The machine runs three subsystems in a closed loop. Each one hands the next a concrete object, and the last one hands its product back to the first. That circuit — not any single component — is the invention.

The three systems never talk through shared mutable state except in one place: the ModuleRegistry. System 3 writes modules into it; the predictor reads modules out of it. Section 02 · SubstrateThe base JEPA circuit

Before anything self-organizes, there is an ordinary JEPA underneath: hide most of an image, encode what is left, and predict the hidden parts' embeddings — never their pixels.

Four components, one loop. The context encoder s_θ sees a few visible blocks. The predictor p_φ is handed those encodings plus positional placeholders for the hidden blocks, and must guess what the hidden blocks would encode to. The target encoder s_ξ — a frozen exponential moving average of the context encoder — provides the answer. The loss is a smooth-L1 in latent space. No pixel decoder exists anywhere in the repository.

E(x, y) = ‖ s_θ(x) − s_ξ(y) ‖² E_pred(x, y) = ‖ p_φ(s_θ(x)) − sg[ s_ξ(y) ] ‖² sg = stop-gradient ξ ← m·ξ + (1−m)·θ m: 0.996 → 1.0

The stop-gradient plus the EMA is the whole anti-collapse story. If gradients flowed into the target encoder, both encoders could agree to output a constant and drive the loss to zero. Freezing the target and moving it only by momentum makes that degenerate solution unreachable on the timescale of a training run.

Design note · why the predictor is narrower

The predictor runs at predictor_embed_dim=96 against an encoder at 192, and projects back up at the end. The bottleneck is deliberate: a predictor with as much capacity as the encoder can memorise rather than predict, and the gap is what forces the encoder to make targets that are actually predictable from context.

Section 03 · SubstrateMasking geometry

The mask is the task. Change the block sizes and you change what the model is being asked to learn — this is the single highest-leverage set of numbers in any config file.

Masks are generated in the dataloader's collate_fn, not in the model. One MaskCollator call produces, per batch: one large target block covering 20–40% of the grid, and four small context blocks covering 15–20% each, sampled from the complement of the target so context and target never overlap. The seed advances once per batch through a process-shared counter, so every worker in a multi-process dataloader produces a different mask layout without colliding.

Section 04 · Subsystem 1Stream Encoder

System 1 is the least glamorous and the most structurally important: it is the adapter that lets Systems 2 and 3 exist without knowing anything about vision transformers.

It does two jobs. First, ContextEncoder hangs a forward hook on every transformer block and records three numbers per layer — mean, std, and mean token norm — so representation flow through the depth of the network is observable. Second, StreamEncoder manufactures the energy_fn closure that System 2 will walk over.

That closure is the pivot of the whole design. System 2's Langevin sampler, blank-space detector and Fisher metric all accept a bare callable [B,D] → [B]. They never import a model. Swap the vision encoder for a graph encoder and the exploration machinery does not notice.

Section 05 · Subsystem 2Energy Explorer

System 2 asks a question no ordinary training loop asks: where on this energy surface is the model uncertain? Then it deliberately walks there and records the path.

Gradient descent moves downhill and stops. System 2 instead runs a Langevin diffusion — downhill drift plus calibrated noise — with the noise turned up in regions the detector flags as blank. The recorded paths are the raw material System 3 will mine.

Blank space H(z) = ∇²_z E(z), blank(z) ⟺ λ_min(H) < τ ∨ Var[p(z+ε)] > τ_v Langevin z_{t+1} = z_t − η ∇_z E(z_t) + √(2η/β) · ε_t, ε ~ N(0, I) Temperature β(z) = β₀ / (1 + blank_score(z)) more blank ⇒ smaller β ⇒ bigger step Fisher metric F(z) = (1/σ²) Jᵀ J, J = ∂p/∂z estimated by finite differences

Stage 1 · Blank-space detection

Two independent signals, OR-ed together. The Hessian route probes curvature: for each of 32 random unit directions d, a three-point stencil estimates the directional second derivative. If the smallest curvature found is near zero, the energy surface is locally flat — the model has no opinion here. The perturbation route jitters the input ten times and measures the variance of the predictor's output; high variance means the predictor is unstable here.

Stage 2 · Langevin walk

Each step drifts down the energy gradient and adds Gaussian noise scaled by √(2η/β). Four guards wrap the gradient computation, and all four exist because the energy function is supplied by a caller and may be arbitrarily badly behaved: non-finite energy, non-differentiable energy, energy that produces no gradient at all, and NaN gradients. Every failure path returns a zero gradient, which degrades the walk to pure diffusion rather than crashing the training run.

The differentiable variant

A second pathway exists for end-to-end training: differentiable_sample runs num_warmup_steps detached steps to get somewhere interesting cheaply, then num_diff_steps steps with create_graph=True so gradients flow back through the exploration itself. The noise is detached but its scale is not — the reparameterisation trick, applied to a sampler.

Stage 3 · Fisher metric

The Fisher information metric measures how sharply the predictor's output responds to movement in latent space. Its trace is a scalar: high means the predictor is sensitive here (well-mapped territory), low means it is insensitive (a second, independent blank-space signal). The Jacobian is estimated with forward differences along up to 16 random directions rather than exactly — D_out × D_in exact columns would dominate the step cost.

Stage 4 · Trajectory capture

Trajectories arrive as [T, B, D] and are immediately flattened to [T·B, D], detached, and moved to CPU. Time-ordering is discarded here — deliberately. System 3 treats the result as an unordered point cloud, because persistent homology cares about the shape the walk traced out, not the order in which it traced it.

Section 06 · Subsystem 3Module Crystallizer

System 3 is where the claim becomes a mechanism. It takes a cloud of points that a random walk left behind, measures its shape, and turns each durable shape into a neural network.

The instrument is persistent homology. Grow a ball of radius ε around every point and let the balls merge; watch which topological features appear and disappear as ε grows from 0 to ∞. A feature that survives across a wide range of ε is real structure; one that appears and dies immediately is sampling noise. That survival span is called persistence, and it is the only quantity that decides whether a module gets built.

Stage 1 · Computing the homology

Three backends, tried in order: giotto-tda, then ripser, then a scipy single-linkage fallback. The fallback is honest about its limits — it recovers H₀ only, because single-linkage clustering is the H₀ filtration but says nothing about loops or voids. A run without a real backend installed will therefore only ever crystallize attractor modules, and the code logs a warning saying so.

Persistent homology is roughly O(N³). The point cloud is subsampled to 500 points with a fixed seed before computation — reproducible, and the difference between a fast step and an unusable one.

Stage 3 · Lifecycle

Modules are not permanent. The registry tracks average loss per module and prunes on two independent criteria: worst-average-loss when over capacity, and staleness when a module has gone unused for more than 2 × pruning_patience epochs. A grace period protects newly created modules from being culled before they have had a chance to train.

Section 07 · Closing the circuitDynamic Predictor

Crystallized modules are useless until something calls them. The DynamicPredictor is the return path — and it is engineered almost entirely around the risk that a brand-new, untrained module destroys a converging model.

Three separate brakes are applied to the module contribution before it reaches the output. A learned scalar α = σ(module_logit) initialised to 0.05, so modules start at 5% influence. A per-token gate — a small sigmoid MLP that decides, position by position, whether the module mixture is relevant at all. And a LayerNorm on the combined module output, so a module that blows up in magnitude cannot swamp the base prediction. Only after all three does the mixture get added as a residual.

Section 08 · Closing the circuitRecursive loop & convergence

The three systems run on three different clocks. RecursiveLoop.step() is the timing chassis that decides which of them fires on any given call, and the convergence monitor is the instrument that says when the loop has stopped producing new structure.

Exploration is expensive; crystallization is more expensive still and needs several trajectories before there is enough of a point cloud to filter. So each stage has its own divisor against a single monotonic iteration counter, and crystallization additionally requires min_trajectories_for_crystallization to have accumulated. The convergence tap alone runs unconditionally, every iteration.

The convergence metric

Three terms, summed. Module formation rate — has the module population stopped changing? Representation drift — measured as a KL divergence between histograms of latent norms across consecutive iterations. Landscape change — the absolute change in energy spread. When the sum stays below epsilon for patience consecutive iterations, the system declares convergence.

C(t) = |M(t) − M(t−1)| / max(M(t), 1) module formation rate + KL( R(t) ‖ R(t−1) ) representation drift, 50-bin norm histogram + |S(t) − S(t−1)| energy landscape smoothness change converged ⟺ C(t) < ε for k consecutive iterations ε = 0.01, k = 5
Read the first iteration carefully

On the very first call there is no previous histogram, so repr_divergence is hard-set to 1.0 (line 76). That guarantees C(0) ≫ ε and prevents a cold system from declaring itself converged before it has done anything. It also means the first entry in the convergence history is not comparable with the rest — the analysis scripts drop it.

Section 09 · OperationsTraining stack

One training step has four decision points where it can refuse to do the normal thing. Every one of them exists to stop a single bad batch from permanently corrupting the EMA target encoder — the one component that cannot be recovered by further gradient descent.

Where the loop attaches to training

The recursive loop runs once per epoch, not per step, using the last batch of that epoch. Newly crystallized modules then have to be introduced to the optimizer — they did not exist when it was constructed. _register_new_module_params adds them as a fresh param group with weight_decay=0.0, tracking which ids it has already seen so a module is never added twice.

Section 10 · OperationsCrystara graph mode

The same three systems, run against graphs instead of images. Only the input layer is replaced — and that replacement is the strongest evidence that the tripartite mechanism is about geometry rather than about vision.

In graph mode a "token" is an entity sitting at a point on a sphere. Four signals compose into its embedding: a 384-dimensional sentence-transformer fingerprint, an S² position encoded by a spherical-harmonic basis, a tangent-space velocity, and eight structural features summarising its causal neighbourhood. Everything downstream — predictor, Langevin walk, persistent homology, module factory, registry — is byte-identical to the vision path.

The repository's own assessment

Quoting results/GRAPH_BENCHMARKS.md directly: "The headline '+20 to +36.6 AUC pts over baseline JEPA' is accurate; 'beats every GNN everywhere' would not be."

GAT and GCN beat Crystara on GDELT by a wide margin. On SEC EDGAR the result is a scaling story rather than an accuracy one — Crystara is the only model that finishes and produces a usable downstream classification number, but 66.4% is not a benchmark-leading figure in absolute terms, and no supervised baseline both scales and classifies well enough to compare against.

The qualitative result

On the CSET semiconductor graph the pipeline crystallized 16 modules that map one-to-one onto real supply-chain clusters, discovered from persistent homology on Langevin trajectories with no labels and no prompting. Nine are named in results/graph_benchmarks/semiconductor_modules.json:

The interpretation the JSON records maps cleanly onto the module vocabulary from Section 06: H₀ attractors pull industry clusters together, H₁ cycles capture feedback loops in the manufacturing pipeline, H₂ boundary modules handle the interfaces between pipeline stages. The last two named modules are the interesting ones — they are dependencies that do not show up as obvious clusters, which is what a topological method should find that a clustering method would not.

Image results — and why they do not hold up

The original single-seed CIFAR-10 ablation showed a 60.8% loss improvement. Multi-seed re-runs at smaller scale did not reproduce it. From results/ANALYSIS.md:

The analysis attributes the gap to model capacity: with embed_dim=192, depth=6 the crystallized modules have enough room to contribute; at embed_dim=64, depth=2 they do not. What does replicate at every scale tested is module formation itself — four modules formed reliably in both experiments across both seeds, and convergence arrived within one to three epochs of the vanilla run. The mechanism works; its benefit on small image models does not survive a seed sweep.

Reading these numbers honestly

The image results are two-seed comparisons at a scale the analysis itself calls too small, and the graph results are single runs per benchmark. Neither set carries the statistical weight a published claim would need. The repository says so; treat the graph deltas as promising and the 60.8% figure as a single-seed artifact.

The single most consequential install

Without giotto-tda or ripser, the crystallizer silently degrades to single-linkage clustering. It logs a warning and keeps running, but two thirds of the module vocabulary become unreachable — you will only ever see mod_attractor_* ids. If a run produces no cycle or boundary modules, check the backend before you change any threshold.
